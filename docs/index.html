                    Harmonization method

                    MODIS's 1 km camera sees far fewer small fires than VIIRS's 375 m one. Joined as they are, the record jumps 3.6× from 2011 to 2012 although nothing changed on the ground. The calibration below removes the camera change so that years can be compared.

                    1. Count fire cell-days. Hotspots are placed on a 0.05° grid. Each cell with a fire detection counts once per day, regardless of how many pixels detect it.
                    2. Calibrate older observations. For each region, a linear model and a power-law model are fitted using the months when both MODIS and VIIRS observed, from February 2012 through 2025.
                    3. Validate across years. Each overlap year is held out in turn. The model with the lower error on held-out years converts the older MODIS counts to the VIIRS scale.
                    4. Measure departures from typical activity. Each month is compared with the median for the same calendar month across 2003–2025.
                    View calibration results →

                    MODIS → VIIRS calibration

                    Each dot is one month in which both sensors observed (Feb 2012–2025). The line is the fitted model used to bring older MODIS months onto the VIIRS scale.

                    Held-out validation. Each overlap year is predicted by a model fitted without it. Across the ten regions the median error is 18.4% per region-month (10.5–29.8% by region), and R² stays between 0.90 and 0.99. These are the numbers to quote as the uncertainty of a calibrated region-month; individual months can be worse.

                    Sumatra, in plain numbers. The driest tenth of three-month spells average 5,783 fire cell-days a month. The wettest tenth average 784. A 7.4× difference, measured on this project's own record.

                    Caveats

                    • Sarawak is the exception: its fire follows the calendar, not how dry the preceding quarter was. Do not claim a rainfall effect there without showing it.
                    • POWER reports monthly rainfall as millimetres per day, not per month. January's 7.6 for Sumatra means about 236 mm for the month. Convert before use or everything is off by roughly 30×.

                    How this was measured

                    Every dataset on this page was tested the same way, and the test is deliberately unkind to the model. A forecast is issued at the end of May for June to November of the same year, using only what is knowable at that moment: January to May of the target year, carried forward. The model learns from earlier years only, and the year being predicted never appears in its training set.

                    Lower is better. The baseline is the seasonal pattern alone: the mean of the previous five years, same month, in log space.

                    Read this before quoting any of it

                    • The old 38.2% headline is gone. It counted all twelve months and used the year's actual El Niño value, which is not known when the forecast is made. This page measures the dry half only, with no foreknowledge. The site's own backtest — the one that picks a model per region — now reports 42.0% for all regions together.
                    • Choosing the winning input per region is mild hindsight. The pick is made after seeing the backtest, which flatters the result. The old use_enso flag worked the same way. It is disclosed here rather than hidden.
                    • Forecasts work at two scales. Malaysia and Indonesia have a regional forecast map. Explorer now gives every country a cell-level model estimate from the global 1° grid: the number of cells with at least a 50% chance of fire, the peak cell chance, and expected fire cell-days. The observed history below it is the satellite record. City searches use the country estimate because the data do not support city forecasts.

                    Limits

                    What the record cannot tell you, in one place.

                    • A region-month is uncertain. The calibration error is a median of 18.4%, and the worst region is 29.8%. The error is a median, so individual months can be worse.
                    • Before 2012 is modelled, not observed. MODIS counts are converted onto the VIIRS scale with a fit validated on the 2012–2025 overlap. A converted value is an estimate with the error above, not a measurement.
                    • The calibration is regional. The coefficients are fitted per region and apply to VIIRS S-NPP. They do not transfer to a different VIIRS satellite or to the world record's NOAA-20 counts.
                    • Cell-days count hot pixels, not area or smoke. A fire cell-day says a 0.05° cell had at least one detection that day. It is not burned hectares, fire intensity or air quality.
                    • Cloud and smoke hide fires. Every sensor misses fires under thick cloud or smoke; the record counts what was seen, not everything that burned.
                    • The forecast's 80% range is optimistic. On held-out Jun–Nov months it contained the outcome 68% of the time, not 80%. Trust the expected value and the relative ranking more than the edges of the range.
                    • The forecast is a statistical outlook, not a warning. It covers ten regions in Malaysia and Indonesia, gives typical monthly activity and relative risk, and is not an official alert.

                    The world record reports country totals by sensor. Explorer also shows historical fire activity on a separate 1° global grid. Read them with the world record.

                    World fire record

                    Per-country monthly fire history for 233 countries, 2003 onwards. It exists so the site can answer questions about anywhere on Earth, not only Malaysia and Indonesia. It is a record, not a forecast.

                    Where it comes from

                    SourceSensorYearsSize
                    NASA FIRMS, all-countries yearly archivesMODIS C6.1 (Terra + Aqua)2003–202523 archives
                    NASA FIRMS, all-countries yearly archivesVIIRS JPSS-1 / NOAA-20, 375 m2018–20258 archives
                    Supplied as Dataset (World).zip: nested zips, 6,474 country-year CSVs4.6 GB zipped, 20.9 GB raw

                    How it was built

                    The archive is never extracted. world_build.py opens each yearly zip from memory, streams every country CSV through a chunked reader, aggregates, and discards the rows. Peak memory stays in the hundreds of megabytes and the only thing written to disk is one small CSV — which matters, because 20.9 GB of raw CSVs does not fit on this machine.

                    The filters are deliberately identical to Clean.py, so a number here means the same thing as a number in the regional record:

                    • vegetation fires only — FIRMS type 0, dropping volcanoes, gas flares and offshore
                    • MODIS confidence 30 or above; VIIRS low-confidence detections dropped
                    • rows missing a position or a timestamp dropped
                    • dates are local, approximated from longitude (offset = round(lon / 15))

                    The two numbers

                    61,111 rows · 233 countries · 224,951,233 detections kept · built in 21 minutes
                    ColumnMeans
                    hotspotsRaw detections, i.e. satellite pixels. Rises when a sensor sees the same fire with more, smaller pixels.
                    fire_cell_daysA 0.05° cell that burned on a given day, counted once. The comparable measure, and the one the site uses.
                    frp_sumTotal fire radiative power in megawatts, a rough proxy for intensity.

                    What it does not do

                    Counts are per sensor and are not calibrated onto a common scale. This is the important caveat. The regional record rescales MODIS onto VIIRS S-NPP using a fit validated over the 2012–2025 overlap. The world archive carries VIIRS JPSS-1 (NOAA-20) instead — a different satellite, with only a 2018–2025 overlap — so those coefficients do not transfer. Compare one country with another within the same sensor; never add MODIS and VIIRS together.

                    • Country-level outlooks. Explorer uses flame pins for every country's model estimate: larger flames mean more grid cells with at least a 50% chance of fire. Malaysia and Indonesia also have the finer regional forecast.
                    • No sub-national detail. Country totals only — there are no province boundaries in this layer.
                    • MODIS before 2018 is alone. Any apparent jump at 2018 is VIIRS arriving, not fire increasing.
                    • Country is taken from the filename as NASA packaged it, not from a boundary test.

                    In the app

                    Any of the 233 countries can be searched and added to the place watchlist, next to the Malaysian and Indonesian areas. World rows are labelled History only and are deliberately drawn differently:

                    • their numbers stay neutral grey — the red/amber risk bands mean forecast risk, which these do not have
                    • the columns show the latest year's fire cell-days and the peak month, not cells at ≥50% and a maximum chance
                    • the fire outlook dial and the share bar hide themselves, because both describe a forecast
                    • the detail panel states that the counts are on a different scale from the regional figures

                    Ziro reads the same record through get_world_country, get_world_ranking and get_world_outlook, so it can answer about any country — and is told to say plainly that it has history and seasonal estimates there, but no forecast.

                    Flags

                    Every country that names a place in the interface carries its national flag — watchlist rows, search results, the detail header and the overview list. flags_build.py resolves each FIRMS country name to an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code and saves that flag as SVG under Frontend/assets/flags/, with index.json as the name → code map the browser reads.

                    They are stored in the project rather than linked from a CDN, so the site works offline, nothing is requested from a third party while someone is using it, and a flag cannot vanish because another service changed.

                    StepResult
                    Names matched automatically218 of 233
                    Hand-checked overrides15 — Cote d Ivoire, Dem Rep Korea, Lao PDR, Swaziland, Macedonia Former Yugoslav Republic of and others FIRMS spells its own way

                    One trap worth recording. The matcher ignores words like "democratic" and "republic" to absorb spelling differences — which collapses Democratic Republic of the Congo and Republic of Congo onto the same name, handing DR Congo the wrong flag. It is the country with the most fire on Earth, so the error sat at the top of the ranking. Both are now pinned explicitly (cd and cg), and the script warns if any two countries ever resolve to one code again.

                    Steps

                    1. Rebuild the fire record.
                      python Clean.py --modis "modis_2000-2025_*.csv" --viirs "viirs-snpp_2012-2025_*.csv" --out Clean_dataset\clean_hotspots.csv
                    2. Recalibrate MODIS to VIIRS.
                      python calibration.py --inp clean_hotspots.csv
                    3. Pull the climate data.
                      python pull_power.py — 28 parallel requests, no key, about six seconds.
                    4. Re-run the rainfall check.
                      python check_power_vs_fire.py — reproduces the numbers on the Climate data page.
                    5. Pull the fire weather.
                      python pull_gfwed.py — 288 monthly files, ten at a time, about ten minutes. Resumable: re-run it and it continues where it stopped.
                    6. Re-run the accuracy test.
                      python eval_predictors.py — reproduces every number on the What the data is worth page.
                    7. Build the world record.
                      python world_build.py --zip "Dataset (World).zip" — streams 6,474 country CSVs out of the nested archives without unpacking them, about 21 minutes for 225 million detections. Add --years 2024,2025 to test on a slice first.
                    8. Fetch the flags.
                      python flags_build.py — resolves every country in the world record to an ISO code and saves its flag under Frontend/assets/flags. A few seconds; re-run with --force to refresh them.

                    All four scripts sit in the project root and use the backend virtual environment.
