02 · The problem

The haze is made upwind

In dry seasons, fires on peat and farmland in Sumatra and Kalimantan send smoke across the Strait of Malacca and into Malaysian Borneo. Malaysia breathes the smoke but cannot see the fires from the ground.

Smoke from Sumatra's hotspots is carried by prevailing winds into Peninsular Malaysia, and from Kalimantan into Sarawak and Sabah.ASMC analysis, via Malaysiakini (2019)
In 2019, haze closed schools in nine districts of Sarawak.Malaysiakini (2019)

03 · Why the records disagree

Two satellites, two different counts

MODIS

  • Terra and Aqua
  • 1 km pixels
  • Record from Nov 2000
  • Satellites nearing end of life

VIIRS

  • Suomi NPP
  • 375 m pixels
  • Record from Jan 2012
  • Sees more small fires

Over the same land from 2012 to 2025, VIIRS logged – as many hotspots as MODIS.

Splice the two records naively and 2012 jumps – over 2011. The fires did not change that year. The satellite did.

04 · Method

From two records to one calendar

  1. IngestNASA FIRMS standard-processing archives: MODIS C6.1 (2000–2025) and VIIRS S-NPP 375 m (2012–2025). The harmonized record starts in 2003, the first full year of Aqua.
  2. CleanKeep vegetation fires (type 0). Drop duplicates, MODIS confidence below 30 and VIIRS low confidence. Use local-time dates.
  3. GridCount fire cell-days on a 0.05° grid: a cell that burns on a given day counts once, however many pixels detect it.
  4. CalibrateFor each of 10 regions across Malaysia and Indonesia, fit a linear and a power-law model from MODIS to the VIIRS scale on the overlap months, Feb 2012 to 2025. The five haze-source and haze-receiving regions are shown.
  5. ValidateLeave one year out, refit, predict that year. Keep the model with the lower error.
Leave-one-year-out validation
RegionModelR²Error
KalimantanPower law0.98910.5%
SumatraPower law0.98211.7%
SabahPower law0.89718.4%
Peninsular MalaysiaLinear0.95718.5%
SarawakLinear0.92421.0%

Error is the median absolute error of calibrated MODIS against observed VIIRS, monthly, on the left-out year (167 overlap months per region).

One fixed factor is not enough. In Kalimantan a single linear factor gives 29.4% error; the power law gives 10.5%.

The same left-out errors were used to choose each model, so they are slightly optimistic. The grid on the map is drawn at 0.1° for legibility; counts use 0.05° cells.

06 · Impact

Who it helps

Is this season's burning unusual? A consistent 23-year baseline can answer that.

ASMC's monthly haze reviews compare hotspot counts with the previous five years, from one VIIRS satellite. Our harmonized record reaches back to 2003.

ASMC Review of Regional Haze Situation, August 2025

The web app also gives a 2026–2027 outlook (El Niño + NASA GFWED drought code). Issued in May and scored on June–November, its typical monthly error is 42% (50% for the seasonal pattern alone). Its ~28 km risk map scores AUC 0.81: the riskiest 10% of cells held 36% of fire days.

Users

Environment and disaster agencies

See early when upwind burning runs above its historical range, and plan the response.

Health and education

Prepare masks, clinic capacity and school plans for the months that historically burn most.

Researchers and land managers

One consistent series across the MODIS-to-VIIRS handover, for peatland and trend studies.

07 · Data, limits and next steps

NASA and partner data

  • NASA FIRMS: MODIS Collection 6.1 active fire, VIIRS S-NPP 375 m active fire (standard processing), country yearly archives
  • NASA GIBS: Blue Marble Next Generation; Blue Marble Shaded Relief and Bathymetry
  • Terrain: AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen Terrarium, SRTM-derived). River lines are flow paths computed from this terrain, not surveyed rivers
  • NASA GISS GFWED fire-weather drought code, for the outlook
  • NOAA CPC Oceanic Niño Index, for the outlook
  • Boundaries: Natural Earth
  • Context: ASMC, Bernama, Malaysiakini

Tools

  • three.js and GSAP (bundled in assets/vendor.js, so the map runs offline), Google Fonts (Rajdhani, IBM Plex)

Limits

  • A hotspot is a hot pixel at overpass time, not one fire
  • Cloud and forest canopy hide some fires
  • Not a haze forecast: wind and rain are not modelled
  • Values before Feb 2012 are calibrated MODIS estimates, with 10% to 30% typical error by region
  • The outlook is a probability from history and climate drivers, not a certainty; picking each region’s best driver after the backtest flatters it slightly
  • Region boxes are approximate

Next steps

  • Add 2026 near-real-time data from the FIRMS API
  • Add NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 VIIRS to the regional calibration
  • Overlay wind and Malaysian air-quality records
